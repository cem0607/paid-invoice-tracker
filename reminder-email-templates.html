5 invoice reminder emails that actually get you paid

What to send when a client goes quiet — at day 3, day 10, day 20 and day 45 — plus the late-fee maths most freelancers never bother with. Copy them, change the brackets, send.

The one rule that matters: stage your follow-ups and stay factual. Most clients who pay late pay somewhere between day 3 and day 10. The tone of the first email is what keeps the relationship intact — warm, specific, no guilt trip.

The late-fee formula (use it, don't guess)

Simple interest, pro-rated by days — the standard way to charge a late fee:

late fee = balance × (annual rate % / 100) × (days late / 365)

Example: $2,500 invoice, 12% annual rate, 45 days late
= 2500 × 0.12 × 45/365
= $36.99

Check your local rules first — some countries cap the interest you may charge, and your contract needs to state it. If your invoice has no late-fee clause, you can usually still add one for future projects.

Three things that reduce late payments more than any email

1. Deposits. 30–50% up front removes the worst of the risk.
2. Short terms. Net-7 or due-on-receipt for small work; reserve net-30 for clients who need it.
3. One-click payment link in every email. Friction is why people "forget".
